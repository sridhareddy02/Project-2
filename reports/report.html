Customer lifecycle and retention lab

Simulated data, seed 21: 17,550 customers, 66,884 orders, 36 months.

All numbers on this page come from a seeded simulation. Nothing here is real customer data, and the win-back experiment's treatment effect is synthetic.

Key findings

  • Champions are 23% of customers but 54% of revenue to date, and 57% of them buy again within 90 days, against 5% for Hibernating customers.
  • K-means on log recency, frequency and monetary value picks 4 clusters and agrees weakly with the four hidden behavioural segments (adjusted Rand index 0.13; 0.13 when forced to 4): spend and frequency overlap between segments.
  • Retention settles near 13% of a cohort ordering in a given month a year after acquisition (month 1: 18%).
  • The churn model, trained on 2024-06-30 and tested on 2025-06-30, reaches AUC 0.70 (logistic regression) and 0.69 (tuned gradient boosting); the riskiest decile churns at 80% versus 57% overall.
  • Predicted six-month value beats a run-rate baseline: MAE 59 versus 90, and the top decile captures 44% of future revenue versus 35%.
  • Holt-Winters forecasts the held-out six months with a MAPE of 1.9%, against 17.3% for a naive forecast.
  • The win-back test measured +2.47 pp (95% CI 1.49 to 3.45, p < 0.001); the Bayesian probability that the offer helps is 100%. 2 of 4 channels show a lift on their own after false-discovery correction, and a formal interaction test finds no evidence that the effect differs by channel (p = 0.51).
  • The testing method checks out in simulation: 5.1% false positives on A/A tests (target 5%), 95.2% interval coverage, and simulated power 98% versus 98% from the formula.

Limitations

  • Simulated customers follow simple purchase processes; real behaviour is messier.
  • The experiment's treatment effect is injected by the simulator, so the lift is known in advance.
  • Forecast accuracy reflects a smooth synthetic series.
